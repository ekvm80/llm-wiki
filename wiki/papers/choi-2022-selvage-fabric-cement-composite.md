# Mechanical Properties of Eco-Friendly High-Ductility Cement Composites Upcycling Industrial-Waste Selvage Short Fibers from High-Performance Ballistic Fabric

## Summary

To upcycle into short fibers for construction composites the high-value selvage — a hybrid yarn of 80 wt.% PE fiber + 20 wt.% SUS 316L metal wire that is generated in large quantities as edge waste during the manufacture of ballistic/stab-resistant high-performance fabric and is currently landfilled or incinerated in its entirety — this study produced four types of short fibers (N3, O3, N6, O6) with cutting length (3 mm vs 6 mm) and mechanical opening treatment in an opening machine as variables, incorporated them into cement paste (W/C 0.35, 1.75 vol.% fiber), and evaluated 28-day compressive strength, uniaxial direct-tension behavior, tensile strain capacity, tensile toughness, and multiple-cracking characteristics (corresponding author Prof. Bang-Yeon Lee of Chonnam National University; co-authors Se-Eon Park, Yun-Yong Kim of Chungnam National University, and Young-Min Kim of the recycling-resources institute). As a result, all composites reinforced with industrial-waste selvage short fibers maintained compressive strengths of 63–66 MPa while exhibiting distinct tensile strain-hardening and saturated fine multiple cracking, developing a tensile elongation of **2.6 %–3.5 %** and a tensile strength of **7.5–9.0 MPa**, demonstrating for the first time worldwide the feasibility of manufacturing a high-performance eco-friendly high-ductility composite capable of replacing expensive virgin fibers.

## Key Contributions

1. **Successful upcycling of industrial-waste hybrid fabric (PE+SUS selvage) into a high-ductility cement composite**:
   - Incorporating waste selvage (a composite of high-strength PE at 3000 MPa + stainless steel wire at 800 MPa) at 1.75 vol.% achieved ultra-high ductility of **2.6–3.5 %**, greatly exceeding the 2.0 % tensile elongation required of standard ECC.
2. **Elucidating the mechanical effects of opening-machine treatment and fiber length**:
   - **Effect of mechanical opening**: the O series (O3, O6), whose bundled fibers were untangled by the opening machine, showed an increased interfacial contact area with the cement paste, improving first-crack strength (4.8–5.1 MPa) and compressive strength (65.5–66.0 MPa).
   - **Effect of fiber length**: the 6 mm short fiber (N6) enlarged the fiber bridging length, pushing the PSH strength performance index ($f_t/f_{1st}$) to **2.05** and achieving the highest tensile strength of **9.0 MPa** and the highest tensile strain capacity of **3.5 %**.
3. **Sustainable resource-circulating eco-friendly materialization for construction**:
   - Establishes a pathway that drastically cuts the greenhouse-gas emissions and production costs incurred in manufacturing expensive virgin PVA/PE short fibers, and re-resources an industrial by-product into a high-value seismic/repair-grade tensile strain-hardening composite.

## Methods

- **Materials and binder composition (Table 1)**:
  - Ordinary Portland cement (OPC, density $3.15\ \text{g/cm}^3$) 1.0.
  - Water (W/C 0.35), polycarboxylate superplasticizer (SP) 0.005, viscosity-modifying agent (VMA, methylcellulose) 0.0003, antifoaming agent 0.001.
- **Composition of the recycled selvage short fibers (Table 2)**:
  - Core: SUS 316L metal wire (tensile strength 700–800 MPa, elongation 28 %, 20 % of total weight).
  - Sheath: high-strength PE fiber (diameter 18 µm, tensile strength 3000 MPa, elastic modulus 120 GPa, specific gravity 0.97, 80 % of total weight).
  - Fiber volume fraction: fixed at 1.75 vol.%.
- **Fiber processing variables (4 types)**:
  - **M-N3**: length 3.77 mm, non-opened.
  - **M-O3**: length 3.65 mm, opened with an opening machine.
  - **M-N6**: length 7.03 mm, non-opened.
  - **M-O6**: length 6.32 mm, opened with an opening machine.
- **Test equipment and methods**:
  - Compressive strength: KS L 5105 (three $50\ \text{mm}$ cube specimens, 28-day water curing).
  - Uniaxial direct-tension test: four JSCE-standard dogbone specimens (central cross-section $30\ \text{mm} \times 13\ \text{mm}$, gauge length 80 mm), 20 kN universal testing machine (displacement control at 0.1 mm/min), two LVDTs installed.
  - Crack-pattern analysis: analysis of the number of cracks within the gauge section after the tension test and of failure modes.

## Results

### 1. 28-day compressive strength and uniaxial direct-tension performance

| Mix ID | Fiber processing form | Mean fiber length (mm) | 28-d compressive strength (MPa) | First-crack strength (MPa) | Ultimate tensile strength (MPa) | Tensile strain capacity (%) | PSH strength ratio ($f_t/f_{1st}$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M-N3** | Non-opened, 3 mm | 3.77 ± 0.95 | 63.3 ± 4.2 | 4.3 ± 0.4 | 7.5 ± 0.5 | 2.6 ± 0.5 % | 1.74 |
| **M-O3** | **Opened, 3 mm** | 3.65 ± 0.80 | **66.0 ± 2.9** | 4.8 ± 0.3 | 8.5 ± 0.6 (+13 %) | 2.8 ± 0.6 % | 1.77 |
| **M-N6** | **Non-opened, 6 mm** | 7.03 ± 0.71 | 63.8 ± 5.3 | 4.4 ± 0.8 | **9.0 ± 1.1 (+20 %)** | **3.5 ± 1.1 %** | **2.05 (highest)** |
| **M-O6** | Opened, 6 mm | 6.32 ± 1.33 | 65.5 ± 2.8 | **5.1 ± 0.2** | 8.9 ± 0.8 | 3.0 ± 0.6 % | 1.74 |

### 2. Energy-absorption toughness and multiple-cracking failure behavior
- All mixes developed tensile strain capacities above 2.6 %, fully satisfying the strain-hardening criteria for high-ductility cement composites (ECC/HPFRCC).
- Mix M-N6, leveraging its long bridging length, recorded the best deformation performance, achieving an ultimate tensile strength of 9.0 MPa and a tensile elongation of 3.5 %.
- The opening treatment (O series) strengthened the fiber–matrix interfacial bond and was proven to raise crack-initiation strength by 11–16 %.

- **Overall conclusion**: when high-performance waste selvage is processed into short fibers and applied to cement composites, it was established that waste recycling and low-cost mass production of a 65 MPa-class compressive-strength, ~3.5 %-class ultra-high-ductility composite can be achieved simultaneously.
